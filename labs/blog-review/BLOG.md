# KCD Around the World: New York

Welcome to KCD Around the World, a new series where we explore Kubernetes Community Days from every corner of the globe. Through conversations with organizers, volunteers, speakers and attendees, we'll share the stories that make each KCD unique.

Our first stop: New York City.

When most people think of a KCD, they think about talks and technical sessions. But behind every event is a community of volunteers, contributors, organizers and attendees who make it all happen.

We caught up with the KCD New York team to learn what made this year's event special.

## Meet the Community

### Tell us a little about yourself and your role in KCD New York.

I'm Christopher Tineo and I work for a government consultant named [Game Plan Tech](gameplan.tech). I was an organizer for KCD NY 2026 where I was mostly working with building our event agenda and our [event website](kcdnewyork.com).

![DSC01641](https://hackmd.io/_uploads/rJGhQHEzzl.jpg)

### How did you first get involved with Kubernetes and the cloud native community?

I went to a meetup organized in Santo Domingo by Victor Recio around Feb 2024 right after I got my CKAD certification. I had the chance to connect with a few engineers who eventualy became organizers for our [CNCF chapter](https://www.linkedin.com/company/cloudnativesdq), Ayesha Yege and Enmanuel Medina.

As I dived deeper into the CNCF community, I knew I wanted to be a part of it. After watching Julia Morgado's inspiring talk at [Kubecon 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rqtENN7iveQ&t=32s&pp=ygUgZnJvbSBub24tdGVjaCB0byBjbmNmIGFtYmFzc2Fkb3I%3D), I was driven to take action. Despite living in the Dominican Republic, I booked a flight to KCD NY 2024 and applied to volunteer. Luckily, I got a spot on the team, and it turned out to be an incredible firsthand experience with the community.

### What motivated you to volunteer or organize KCD New York?

NYC has been the start of all the great things that came after that conference in 2024 so I wanted to be that person that could motivate more engineers from my country to improve their careers and knowing more from the Cloud Native community (which was not as developed back in the day).

That motivated me to be a speaker last year and this year I had the privilege of being an organizer.

## What Makes KCD New York Special?

### Every KCD has its own personality. What makes the New York community unique?

What I love about KCD New York is its unique ability to bring together engineers from all over the world. That rich multiculturalism gives it the distinct energy of a mini-KubeCon.

![DSC01271](https://hackmd.io/_uploads/BkV24HEzMg.jpg)

### What was the atmosphere like on event day?

Organizing my first KCD this year was an entirely new experience. I underestimated the effort required to pull off an event of this scale, but by the end of the day, seeing both speakers and attendees thanking us for the conference and having the chance to meet some of our speakers was incredibly rewarding.

### Was there a moment that made you stop and think, "This is why we do this"?

The closing remarks were really emotive for me. Seeing my friends from Cloud Native Santo Domingo, Ayesha and Miguel, my mentor, Victor, taking the stage as a speaker was surreal, just a few years ago this was a dream for me and witnessing this becoming a reality made me feel proud of how far we’ve come. Seeing them thrive in their careers is far more rewarding to me than any personal achievement from the event.

## Beyond the Talks

### What conversations were happening in the hallways between sessions?

I really had the chance to catch up with some old friends from the community like Chad Crowell and Diana Todea. But I really enjoyed connecting with Mauricio Salatino after I've followed him from a long time.

### Did you meet anyone with an interesting story?

Someone that I really enjoyed talking with was [Daniel Giszpenc](https://github.com/Daniel-Giszpenc). While he doesn't have a ton of experience yet, I was impressed that he joined the Kubernetes project as a maintainer for SIG Security that early in his career. 

He's an example that to become a good mentee you have to be a good engineer first and demostrate that you're putting the effort for what you want to accomplish. I can't wait to see himself excel in his career in the near future.

### What topics generated the most excitement this year?

AI Agents in production, Platform Engineering and Runtime Security.

## Looking Ahead

### For someone considering their first KCD, what would you tell them?

Please check out the upcoming [KCDs on the CNCF website](https://www.cncf.io/kcds/). If there isn't an edition near you and it’s within your means, I highly recommend traveling to one. Traveling has been one of the most transformative experiences for me not only has it allowed me to experience incredible new cultures, but more importantly, it completely shifted my perspective on our industry. It helped me build a global network that I never could have created within my home country.

### What's next for the New York Kubernetes community?

Please join #cloud-native-new-york on [CNCF Slack](https://slack.cncf.io), our [ocgroups.dev chapter](https://ocgroups.dev/cncf/group/5pcu8cb) and follow us on [Linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/company/cloud-native-nyc/) where we would be publishing our upcoming meetups. 

For newcomers, I encourage you to join our meetups and reach out to us for opportunities for making our meetups better either helping promoting them in social media, connecting us with speakers or looking for venues to host them!
